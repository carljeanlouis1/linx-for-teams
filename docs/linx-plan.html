LINX for Teams

See what your training budget is really buying, and test decisions before you make them.

An AI twin of your workforce predicts where leadership's assumptions are wrong. A five-question pulse to real employees confirms it. Leaders see the gap in dollars, then use the twin to simulate “what if” decisions before spending the next one.

Working name. We may rename the product.

The problem

Leaders fund training based on assumptions nobody checks

$1,254spent per employee on training each year
8%of CEOs see business impact from it
75%of learners in our study quit at least one course

Leaders assume people finish courses and quit because the material is hard. Prachi's research found something else: people quit when the course doesn't match who they are trying to become, and many enroll because of pressure from managers or peers. Flashpoint's study plan calls this the perception gap. Engagement tools don't measure it, and skills tools don't explain it.

How it works

Six steps, from assumptions to decisions

1

Map the workforce

The company uploads an anonymous headcount mix: departments, levels, tenure, locations. No names. LINX builds a matching AI twin through Flashpoint.

2

Capture leadership's assumptions

Leaders answer the matched questions first: expected completion, why people quit, why they enroll, which skills matter most.

3

Twin forecast

The twin answers the employee version in minutes. Leaders get an early read on likely gaps, and the survey gets tested before anyone real sees it.

4

Five-question real pulse

Real employees get a short survey through Flashpoint's email distribution. The twin handles the long exploratory questions so people aren't over-surveyed.

5

Three-way gap, in dollars

Leadership belief vs. twin forecast vs. employee reality, translated into training spend at risk. A deck is generated for the leadership team.

6

What-if simulatornew

Leaders test policy and investment ideas on the twin before rolling them out. Every real pulse recalibrates the twin, so the simulations get more accurate each quarter.

Twin vs. reality

New feature

What-if simulator

Leaders ask the twin about a decision before making it. LINX runs the scenario on the twin through Flashpoint, shows the predicted change with the twin's accuracy score next to it, and suggests a short real pulse to confirm the result if the decision is big.

“What if managers stop requiring enrollment?”

Predicts the change in completion and enrollment when pressure is removed.

“What if we fund a data-skills track for operations?”

Shows who signs up, who drifts, and the expected completion by learner type.

“What if courses are tied to a named career path?”

Tests Prachi's identity finding: does clarity about a future role raise completion?

“What happens to engineering if we cut the budget 20%?”

Shows which teams and skills lose the most, before the cut lands.

This is what makes LINX a tool leaders open every month, not a report they read once. Each answer is labeled as a forecast until a real pulse confirms it.

The population problem

Making the twin look like the company

  • Match the mix. The twin is built from the company's own headcount breakdown, not a generic panel. Flashpoint scores how well each synthetic panel fits its target population.
  • Calibrate every round. Each real pulse is used to recalibrate the twin, so it gets closer to that specific company over time.
  • Show the accuracy. The match score is always visible. If it is low, LINX says so and leans on real data.
  • Later: connect real systems. Headcount from Workday or BambooHR, completion from the learning platform. Surveys are then needed only for the “why” questions.

Business case

Why companies would pay

Buyer

CHROs and L&D leaders at companies of 500+ people with a training budget. The CFO cares about the dollar gap.

Pricing (starting idea)

Annual subscription per employee, plus research credits for real pulses and paid panels. A free 10-minute snapshot (three questions plus a twin forecast) acts as the sales hook.

Why us

Culture Amp, Lattice and Viva Glint measure engagement. Degreed and Workday track skills. None shows leaders where their own assumptions are wrong, forecasts results before a survey, or lets them simulate a decision.

Moat

Each company's twin gets more accurate with every pulse. A competitor starts from zero.

Privacy: results are shown only in groups of 5 or more, with no names, and synthetic answers are always labeled and never counted as real employees.

Risks and answers

What we'll be asked

  • “Why trust fake employees?” The twin is a forecast, always shown with its accuracy score, and confirmed by a real pulse.
  • “We already run engagement surveys.” LINX is a training ROI and planning tool, and it means fewer surveys, not more.
  • “Enterprise HR sales are slow.” The free snapshot shortens the first conversation.
